How CivInt Canada works

CivInt is a data pipeline that collects Canadian government information from official public sources every day — committee meetings, lobbying reports, procurement contracts — and connects the dots so you can see the full picture.

What we track

🏛️ Parliamentary Committees

All 34 House of Commons committees are ingested daily from ourcommons.ca. Every meeting transcript, witness list, and committee report is parsed into structured data.

🔒 In-camera detection: The evidence XML schema includes an <InCameraNote/> field — when a committee goes closed-door, this field is populated. CivInt flags these meetings automatically so you know which proceedings are happening off the public record.

💰 Lobbying Registry

The Office of the Commissioner of Lobbying publishes monthly CSV dumps of every registered lobbying communication. CivInt ingests these from the open.canada.ca CKAN portal — showing which companies are meeting with which government departments, on what topics, and how much they're spending.

📄 Government Procurement

Contract awards from CanadaBuys and proactive disclosure datasets are fetched daily. Each contract is tagged with whether it was sole-source or competitive. Large sole-source awards are flagged for review.

👔 Executive Statements

We track 17 high-impact Canadian executives across banking, telecom, and media — the CEOs of RBC, Telus, Bell, Rogers, CBC, Postmedia, Globe and Mail, and more. Their public statements, press releases, and committee appearances are collected from company IR feeds and cross-referenced with our committee data.

🌐 Influence Actors

34 influence actors — international organizations, NGOs, think tanks, and consultancies — are monitored from the UN and WEF to McKinsey and the CCPA. We track their government funding, committee appearances, lobbying activities, and CRA charity data from charities.gc.ca.

📜 Canada Gazette

Regulatory activity from gazette.gc.ca is ingested daily. Part I captures proposed regulations open for public comment; Part II contains enacted regulations and orders-in-council. Each entry is parsed for the regulating department, legal authority, and comment deadlines so you can track regulatory changes before they take effect.

🏛️ Senate Committees

All 20 standing Senate committees are ingested from sencanada.ca using the same XML evidence schema as the House. Meeting transcripts, witness lists, and reports are normalized to the same committee data model, enabling cross-chamber analysis of witness appearances and policy topics.

👑 GIC Appointments

Governor-in-Council appointments — the federal patronage system — are collected from the open.canada.ca GIC dataset. Every order-in-council appointment to boards, commissions, tribunals, and Crown corporations is tracked, including position, term length, remuneration, and location data for patronage mapping.

🗳️ Election Advertising

Third-party election advertising spending is collected from Elections Canada. The module tracks registered third parties, their advertising expenses during election periods, and donor contributions — shedding light on dark money in Canadian federal elections.

🏢 Provincial Lobbying

The Ontario lobbying registry is ingested daily from lobbying.ontario.ca and normalized to the federal lobbying schema. This gives a combined federal-provincial lobbying view, with entity resolution linking lobbyists and organizations across both jurisdictions.

📋 Oversight Bodies

Five oversight streams are monitored: Office of the Auditor General (OAG) performance audits, Parliamentary Budget Officer (PBO) cost estimates, Ethics Commissioner investigation reports and compliance agreements, Competition Bureau merger reviews and enforcement actions, and CRTC decisions and policy rulings. Each is fetched from the respective body's publications feed.

⚖️ Court Decisions

Federal Court, Supreme Court of Canada, and Tax Court decisions are ingested via the CanLII API. Every decision is parsed for case metadata (docket number, court, judge, parties) and full-text content, enabling cross-referencing of legal challenges against government actions, regulations, and procurement disputes.

👷 Labour Market Impact Assessments (LMIA)

Employment and Social Development Canada publishes quarterly lists of every employer who received a positive or negative LMIA through the Temporary Foreign Worker Program. CivInt ingests these from the open.canada.ca CKAN portal — showing which companies are hiring foreign workers, in which occupations, at what wage levels, and in what regions. Data available from 2014 to present.

Cross-reference: LMIA records can be matched against the corporate module (which tracked companies are using TFW programs?) and procurement (companies with government contracts AND LMIA approvals — a potential labour cost story).

How the pipeline works

All 13 data source modules feed into a unified 4-stage pipeline:

  1. Ingestion — Each of the 13 modules has a dedicated fetcher that downloads data from its government source (XML, CSV, RSS, JSON, HTML, API) and funnels it into the pipeline
  2. Parsing — Raw data is cleaned and structured into typed records (committees, lobbying records, contracts, statements, regulations, appointments, court decisions, etc.)
  3. Verification — Every record gets a SHA-256 content hash for independent citation verification and provenance tracking
  4. Cross-referencing — The entity resolution engine links people and organizations across all 13 sources, detecting patterns (revolving doors, lobbying overlaps, regulatory capture, etc.) that become signals

Accessing the data

Dashboard

The CivInt Dashboard gives you a live view of all pipelines, recent signals, and searchable data across all sources. No account needed — it's open to anyone on the local network.

MCP Server (for AI tools)

CivInt speaks the MCP protocol, which means you can connect it to ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, Grok, or any MCP-compatible client. See the AI Tools page for setup instructions per platform.

CLI

Each ingestion module has a command-line interface for manual runs and scripting:

pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core committees --ingest-all
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core senate --ingest-all
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core gazette --ingest-all
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core lobbying --ingest
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core provincial-lobbying --ingest
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core procurement --check-sole-source
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core gic-appointments --ingest
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core election-ads --ingest
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core oversight --ingest-all
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core courts --ingest
pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core influence --ingest-all
pnpm --filter @civint/engine sweep --full

REST API

The API server exposes data over HTTP at https://civint.lan/api/v1/ (internal network). OpenAPI spec available.

Architecture

CivInt runs on your own K3s cluster. The data never leaves your infrastructure. All code is open source under AGPL-3.0 / MIT.

civint/
├── packages/
│ ├── pipeline-core/ # Data ingestion, verification, audit
│ ├── engine/ # Entity resolution, graph, signals, briefs
│ ├── api-server/ # REST API gateway
│ ├── mcp-server/ # MCP protocol server for AI tools
│ ├── portal/ # This website (static HTML)
│ └── pretext-civic/ # Chart component library
├── flake.nix # Nix build for container deployment
└── ROADMAP.md # Full development roadmap

The pipeline runs via cron jobs on the K3s cluster, orchestrated through the engine's sweep scheduler. Each module is independent — you can run committees without procurement, or influence without lobbying.

For developers

CivInt is open source and self-hosted. The entire stack runs on your own infrastructure.

Repository

github.com/reverb256/civint — monorepo with all packages.

Quick start

git clone https://github.com/reverb256/civint
cd civint
pnpm install
pnpm run build

Run an ingestion module

pnpm --filter @civint/pipeline-core build
node -e "import { ingestAllCommittees } from '@civint/pipeline-core';
  ingestAllCommittees(null, { onProgress: console.log });"

MCP server (standalone)

cd packages/mcp-server
pnpm start

Engine sweep

pnpm --filter @civint/engine build
pnpm --filter @civint/engine sweep --full
